State-Level Rurality, Socioeconomic Deprivation, and Medicaid Uptake of SGLT2 Inhibitors Observational Ecological Panel Study 


Background and Rationale

Why SGLT2 inhibitors?
SGLT2 inhibitors (sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors) represent one of the most
important therapeutic advances in cardiovascular and renal medicine in recent decades.
Beyond their original role in glycaemic control, landmark trials — DAPA-HF (2019),
EMPEROR-Reduced (2020), DAPA-CKD (2020), and EMPEROR-Preserved (2021) —
established dapagliflozin and empagliflozin as guideline-directed medical therapies for
heart failure (HF) and chronic kidney disease (CKD), independent of diabetes status. These
indications received formal FDA approval between 2020 and 2022, substantially expanding
the eligible patient population.
Despite compelling evidence, real-world uptake of SGLT2 inhibitors remains far below
guideline-recommended levels, particularly in populations with limited healthcare access.
Guideline-to-practice gaps are widest in patients who stand to benefit most: those with
established HF and CKD.

Why Medicaid?
Medicaid insures approximately 90 million Americans and disproportionately covers
individuals who are low-income, rural, disabled, or from racial/ethnic minority backgrounds
— exactly the populations carrying the highest burden of HF and CKD. Medicaid data
therefore provide a unique window into access inequities that would be invisible in
commercially insured cohorts. Formulary restrictions, prior authorisation requirements, and
prescriber unfamiliarity may further suppress uptake in Medicaid populations relative to
private payers.

Why rurality and deprivation?
Rural Americans face worse cardiovascular outcomes, lower rates of specialist access,
fewer cardiologists and nephrologists per capita, and greater socioeconomic disadvantage
than their urban counterparts. Socioeconomic deprivation compounds these barriers
through cost-related non-adherence, lower health literacy, and reduced patient-provider
contact. If SGLT2 inhibitor adoption is unevenly distributed across states according to
rurality and deprivation, this represents a measurable, modifiable equity gap — and a policy-
actionable finding.

Why now?
The period 2020–2025 is the natural experiment window: FDA approvals for HF and CKD
indications arrived in a discrete, dateable sequence. If uptake diverged by state rurality or
deprivation specifically after these approvals, this implicates structural access barriers
rather than differences in underlying disease burden. This cannot be demonstrated with
claims or EMR data at the individual patient level using freely available national data — but
Medicaid State Drug Utilisation Data (SDUD) make a state-level ecological test feasible,
rapid, and policy-relevant.

Research Question
Primary: Did state-level rurality and socioeconomic deprivation independently predict lower
Medicaid uptake of SGLT2 inhibitors in the post-HF/CKD-indication era (2020–2025), after
adjusting for Medicaid expansion status, state demographics, and temporal trends?

To examine whether state-level rurality and socioeconomic deprivation are associated with
lower Medicaid SGLT2 inhibitor prescribing rates in the period following FDA approvals for
heart failure and CKD indications.

Specific Objectives
Describe national trends in Medicaid SGLT2 inhibitor prescribing per 1,000 enrollees
from 2015 (or 2020) to 2025, stratified by era (pre- and post-2020).
Quantify the association between state rurality quintile and quarterly SGLT2 inhibitor
prescribing rate per 1,000 Medicaid enrollees, adjusting for Medicaid expansion status
and key covariates.
Quantify the association between state deprivation (Social Vulnerability Index or
poverty rate) and SGLT2 inhibitor prescribing, independently and in combination with
rurality.
Test whether uptake divergence by rurality/deprivation widened or narrowed after
the 2020–2022 HF/CKD indication expansions using interaction terms (post-2020 ×
rurality quintile).
Map state-level variation in SGLT2 prescribing rates at the most recent available year
and identify outlier states — including high-deprivation states with unexpectedly good
uptake — to generate hypotheses about enabling factors.

Primary Hypothesis
States with higher proportions of non-metropolitan population (greater rurality) will have
lower quarterly SGLT2 inhibitor prescribing rates per 1,000 Medicaid enrollees in the post-
2020 era, after adjustment for Medicaid expansion status, year-quarter fixed effects, and
state-level covariates.

Secondary Hypotheses
H2: States with higher Social Vulnerability Index scores (greater deprivation) will have
lower SGLT2 prescribing rates, independent of rurality.

Study Design
Design: Ecological repeated cross-sectional study with state-quarter as the unit of analysis,
structured as a state-year-quarter panel model.

Rationale:
This is the correct design for Medicaid SDUD because:
SDUD reports prescriptions aggregated at the state level, not patient level. Individual-
level analysis is not possible.
Repeated quarterly observations allow analysis of temporal trends, pre/post
comparisons, and panel regression with fixed effects.
The ecological design is well-established in health services research using aggregate
drug utilisation data and is routinely published in high-impact cardiovascular journals.
The panel structure (51 states × multiple quarters) provides adequate statistical power
for adjusted regression despite the modest number of state-level units.

7. Study Period
Primary Analysis Period: Q1 2020 – Q4 2024 (or Q2 2025 if available)
Rationale: This captures:
Q1 2020 as the pre-dapagliflozin HF-indication baseline (approval: May 2020)
Full rollout of dapagliflozin HF (2020) and CKD (2021), and empagliflozin HF (~2021–
2022) indications
COVID-19 pandemic disruption as a confounder to be addressed in sensitivity analysis
At least 4–5 post-indication years for trend analysis

Extended Analysis: Q1 2015 – Q4 2019 as Pre-Expansion Baseline
Including 2015–2019 enables:
Characterisation of baseline prescribing during the diabetes-indication-only era
A formal pre/post interrupted time series analysis with the 2020 indication as the policy
shock
Assessment of whether rurality/deprivation gaps in SGLT2 prescribing existed pre-2020
(which would suggest formulary/cost barriers) or emerged after 2020 (which would
suggest indication-adoption barriers)
